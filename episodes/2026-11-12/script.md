# Pharma and Biotech Daily, 2026-11-12

Good morning from Pharma and Biotech Daily, the briefing on what matters in pharma and biotech. It's Thursday, November 12.

Today, the FDA adds a vision warning to every GLP-1 medicine, and Compass Pathways files its psilocybin therapy for review.

We start with the biggest drug class in the market. The FDA has told makers of GLP-1 receptor agonists to update their prescribing information with a possible risk of non arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, known as NAION, a rare condition that can cause sudden loss of vision. The agency's review began with case reports in patients taking these drugs. Animal data did not explain a link, but a meta analysis of many clinical trials found a higher rate of cases among people on treatment. The FDA says events have been seen with every marketed GLP-1 drug. As BioPharma Dive notes, the language describes a potential increased risk of a rare event. For prescribers and payers it adds a counseling point, and for companies it becomes a class label item across obesity and diabetes.

Next, psychedelics. Compass Pathways says it has completed its new drug application to the FDA for COMP360, a synthetic form of psilocybin, in treatment resistant depression. In clinical testing, six weeks of treatment was linked to an average reduction of three point eight points on a sixty point depression severity scale. Compass holds a Commissioner's National Priority Voucher and the FDA granted its request for a rolling review, both of which can shorten the timeline. The filing puts a psychedelic medicine for depression under formal FDA review, with a faster path than usual.

In vaccines, AstraZeneca has acquired Omniose, the startup it has worked with on vaccines since January twenty twenty four. Terms were not disclosed. Omniose brings bioconjugation, a way of making conjugate vaccines that links a carrier protein to an antigen, which the company says can produce higher quality shots. AstraZeneca plans to apply it to shots against serious bacterial infections. Fierce Biotech also covered the deal. Buying a partner outright signals that AstraZeneca wants this platform in house.

A smaller deal now. Abeona Therapeutics has bought a Phase three ready treatment for eosinophilic esophagitis from Ellodi Pharmaceuticals, a startup formed by TPG Capital. The drug, once called APT-1011 and now ABO-801, is an oral form of the steroid fluticasone that dissolves in the mouth and coats the esophagus. Abeona paid twenty million dollars in shares and agreed to pay up to sixty million more if the drug is approved and reaches sales targets. At the same time it raised thirty million dollars in a private placement. The structure keeps most of the cost tied to success, a common shape for late stage asset deals.

On the platform side, Valo Health and Montai Therapeutics are merging. Both were founded in twenty nineteen inside Flagship Pioneering, and the combined company keeps the Valo name. Brian Alexander, Valo's chief executive, will lead it, while Montai's co founder and chief executive, Margo Georgiadis, stays on as an advisor. Montai adds MTAI-1025, an oral NRF2 agonist in Phase one for ulcerative colitis, and an oral STAT6 inhibitor for atopic dermatitis. Valo's partnered work with Novo Nordisk and Merck KGaA continues. No financial terms were shared, and the release came via BioSpace. Combining two platforms under one roof is a way to share data, staff and cash.

Finally, a setback. Zura Bio will stop developing tibulizumab in hidradenitis suppurativa after the antibody missed the primary goal of a Phase two trial. Neither of two doses significantly reduced abscesses and inflammatory nodules after sixteen weeks, and secondary goals also failed. BioPharma Dive reports the shares fell by more than two thirds, leaving the company valued at about ninety three million dollars, below the one hundred and eighty eight million dollars in cash it held at the end of September. Zura plans to keep testing the drug in other immune diseases.

The thread today is risk at both ends of the cycle: regulators revisiting widely used drugs, and young programs living or dying on a single readout.
