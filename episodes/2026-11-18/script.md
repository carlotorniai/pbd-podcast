# Pharma and Biotech Daily, 2026-11-18

Good morning from Pharma and Biotech Daily, the briefing on what matters in pharma and biotech. It's Wednesday, November 18.

Today, GSK wins an early FDA nod for its new lung cancer drug, and Isomorphic Labs sets a record for a private biotech raise.

We start with the regulator. GSK says the FDA has granted accelerated approval to Nelkarmi, known generically as neladalkib, for adults with advanced ALK positive non small cell lung cancer whose disease has already been treated. The decision came ahead of the November twenty seventh target date. It rests on the ALKOVE one study of two hundred twenty two patients. In those who had only received alectinib before, forty nine percent responded, and in more heavily treated patients the figure was thirty two percent. Liver toxicity was the most common side effect. The drug came to GSK through its purchase of Nuvalent, so this is an early return on that bet. A Phase three trial called ALKAZAR is testing it in patients who have not yet had an ALK drug.

Now to money. BioSpace reports that Isomorphic Labs, the London based artificial intelligence drug designer owned by Alphabet, has raised a five billion dollar Series C. Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital led the round, with Alphabet investing again. That is the largest private raise the sector has seen, more than double the two point one billion dollars Isomorphic collected in May, and it brings its total to at least seven point seven billion. The striking part is that the company has not yet named a clinical candidate. It points to preclinical work in oncology and immunology and to partnerships with Novartis, Eli Lilly and Johnson and Johnson. Investors are paying up front for a platform, and the clinic will now have to justify that price.

Lilly, meanwhile, keeps building its cancer pipeline. BioSpace reports that it signed a multi target oncology licensing deal with Stipple Bio, announced alongside its Calytrix Bio buyout, which we covered yesterday. Stipple's Pointillist platform looks for binders to surface markers found only on tumor cells, with the goal of hitting cancer while sparing healthy tissue. Lilly takes worldwide rights and makes an equity investment, plus an undisclosed upfront payment. Stipple could earn up to five point nine billion dollars in development and sales milestones. The young company raised a one hundred million dollar Series A in June to move its first antibody drug conjugate, STP one hundred, toward early trials, and it already has a conjugate collaboration with Lonza. The pattern is clear: Lilly is turning its obesity revenue into a steady stream of early stage bets.

Finally, a mixed result in Alzheimer's disease. AriBio presented topline data from its Phase three POLARIS AD trial of AR1001, a once daily oral pill that works as a brain penetrant PDE5 inhibitor. In all one thousand five hundred thirty five patients, the drug did not separate from placebo on the CDR sum of boxes score at one year. The trial's design allowed success in either of two prespecified groups, and in six hundred four patients not taking other Alzheimer's drugs, decline slowed by thirty one percent. Diarrhea and dizziness were more common on the drug, and more patients stopped because of side effects. AriBio will take the full data to the FDA before deciding next steps. Regulators tend to look hard at subgroup wins, so expect a careful conversation.

The thread today is conviction under uncertainty: a single arm approval, a platform without a drug in the clinic, a milestone heavy licensing deal and a subgroup result, each asking someone to bet before the full picture is in.
