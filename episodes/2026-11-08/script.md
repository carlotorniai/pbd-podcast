# Pharma and Biotech Daily, 2026-11-08

Good morning from Pharma and Biotech Daily, the briefing on what matters in pharma and biotech. It's Sunday, November 8.

Since Friday, a safety signal has emerged for one of the best known gene therapies, and the FDA has opened a new front for an antibody from Johnson and Johnson.

We start with Novartis. The company confirmed that nine patients with spinal muscular atrophy developed tumors after receiving Zolgensma, its one time gene therapy. In two of those cases, the viral vector used to deliver the treatment appears to have inserted itself into the patients' DNA. BioPharma Dive reports that Novartis notified health authorities after each case. About six thousand people have received the therapy, and it brought in about one point one billion dollars in the first nine months of this year. A leading United States patient group says one of the three tumors tested so far looks unrelated to treatment. The broader point is that vector integration has been a theoretical risk in the prescribing information. Now there are real cases to study, and every developer of A A V gene therapies will be asked how it monitors patients over the long term.

In oncology, the FDA granted accelerated approval to Rybrevant Faspro, the under the skin version of amivantamab from Janssen, for recurrent or metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma after platinum chemotherapy and immunotherapy. In a trial of one hundred and two patients, forty two percent responded, against roughly twenty percent or less for current options, according to the agency. Confirmatory studies are required. For J and J, it extends the drug well beyond lung cancer.

In obesity, Eli Lilly will sell Zepbound and its oral drug Foundayo through the telehealth platform Hims and Hers, at the same self pay prices offered elsewhere, starting at one hundred and forty nine dollars a month for Foundayo. BioPharma Dive notes that Hims sold compounded copies of these medicines until a legal challenge from Novo Nordisk earlier this year. Today both leading makers sell through the same channel, which shows how far direct to consumer distribution has moved into the mainstream.

Merck and Moderna reported a manufacturing milestone for their personalized cancer vaccine, intismeran autogene. In the Phase three melanoma trial with more than eleven hundred patients, ninety nine percent of those assigned to the vaccine received a custom made product, according to BioSpace. The combination with Keytruda had already cut the risk of recurrence or death by forty two percent. Proving that individual vaccines can be produced reliably at this scale answers one of the main doubts about the approach. Fierce Biotech adds that Moderna has paused enrollment in its norovirus vaccine program after a Phase three miss and will discuss next steps with regulators.

Agios is cutting more than one hundred research jobs, over a third of its R and D staff, while the FDA reviews mitapivat for sickle cell disease. The agency extended the review to February first, twenty twenty seven, after asking for more information on a possible safety program. Agios says it remains ready to launch and expects to be profitable in twenty twenty eight. Revenue from the mitapivat franchise still grew twenty four percent from the prior quarter, to about fifty five million dollars. Its case shows how a three month regulatory delay quickly changes spending plans at a commercial stage biotech.

Finally, policy. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services finalized a rule that makes Medicare drug price negotiation permanent, carrying the process into twenty twenty nine and beyond, Fierce Pharma reports. The rule also tightens how combination products are classified, to stop companies from steering patients toward new formulations to avoid negotiated prices, and sets a temporary price floor for some small biotech drugs. The industry and its trade group PhRMA have challenged the program repeatedly, so far without success.

The thread running through today's news is proof at scale. Gene therapies, personalized vaccines and new sales channels all have to show they work outside the controlled setting where they were born.
