# Pharma and Biotech Daily, 2026-11-16

Good morning from Pharma and Biotech Daily, the briefing on what matters in pharma and biotech. It's Monday, November 16.

Since Friday, the FDA has turned down Summit Therapeutics' lung cancer antibody ivonescimab, and Europe's drug committee has backed AstraZeneca's blood pressure pill Baxfendy along with two lung cancer drugs that came out of China.

An update on the story we covered last Monday. Summit said on Sunday that the FDA issued a complete response letter for ivonescimab, its P D one and V E G F bispecific antibody partnered with Akeso, filed with chemotherapy for E G F R mutated lung cancer. According to the company release, carried by BioSpace, the agency said a progression free survival benefit without a statistically significant overall survival gain did not establish effectiveness. Summit points to a later analysis with a hazard ratio of zero point seven six, but that came after the primary test. Summit gave no resubmission plan. The verdict keeps the whole P D one and V E G F class off the US market for now, and sets a high bar for overall survival.

Over in Amsterdam, the European Medicines Agency's human medicines committee, the C H M P, published the results of its November meeting on Friday. The headline new medicine is Baxfendy, the brand name for baxdrostat from AstraZeneca, recommended for adults with hypertension. Baxdrostat blocks aldosterone synthase, the enzyme that makes aldosterone, a hormone that raises blood pressure. A positive opinion goes to the European Commission, which usually issues a final decision within about two months. For AstraZeneca, it is an important European step for a newer kind of blood pressure medicine, one aimed at a very large group of patients.

The same committee meeting also cleared a path for two lung cancer medicines with roots in China. Aumseqa, or aumolertinib, an E G F R inhibitor that Fierce Pharma links to Hansoh, won a positive opinion for non small cell lung cancer. Fierce describes the result as a second chance for the drug in Europe. The committee also backed Zybrivo, or sintilimab, a P D one antibody filed by Fortvita Biologics, for non squamous non small cell lung cancer. Together they show Chinese developed oncology drugs moving into Western markets through Europe, even as US regulators remain demanding about trial data.

Not every company left the meeting happy. On re-examination, the committee confirmed its refusal of Meplyffa, arimoclomol from Zevra, for Niemann Pick disease type C, so the drug will not reach European patients on this application. Alexion, part of AstraZeneca, withdrew its application for anselamimab in kappa light chain amyloidosis. On the positive side, Roche's Gazyvaro, obinutuzumab, gained a recommendation for adults with moderate to severe active lupus. The agency also flagged a safety letter for Mounjaro, advising women on oral contraceptives to add a barrier method for four weeks after starting the drug and after each dose increase.

Finally, a gene editing reset. BioSpace reports that French biotech Cellectis may cut up to seventy percent of its global staff, about one hundred sixty people. The cuts follow a September decision to leave allogeneic CAR T behind and focus on in vivo gene editing for chronic disease. The lead candidates are HEAL one oh one, a base editor aimed at A P O C three for severe hypertriglyceridemia, and HEAL two oh one, an epigenetic editor targeting P C S K nine. Both will start in investigator led trials in China, with first data due in the second half of twenty twenty seven. Cellectis had one hundred forty nine million dollars at the end of September, which it expects to last into the second half of twenty twenty eight.

The thread today is evidence. Regulators on both sides of the Atlantic are asking hard questions about what a drug truly delivers, and companies are reshaping their pipelines to give clear answers.
