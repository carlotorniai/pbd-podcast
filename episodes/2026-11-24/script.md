# Pharma and Biotech Daily, 2026-11-24

Good morning from Pharma and Biotech Daily, the briefing on what matters in pharma and biotech. It's Tuesday, November 24.

Today, Eli Lilly adds a Huntington's disease program to its long run of acquisitions, and Novartis reports a positive cardiovascular outcomes result for its cholesterol drug Leqvio.

We start with Lilly. BioPharma Dive reports that Lilly has agreed to buy LoQus23 Therapeutics of Cambridge, England, for up to seven hundred twenty five million dollars in cash. That combines an undisclosed upfront payment with milestone earnouts. LoQus grew out of the Dementia Discovery Fund at SV Health Investors. The prize is LQT-23, an oral small molecule that blocks a DNA repair protein called MSH3, which can worsen the repeat expansions behind Huntington's disease. The drug has not yet entered human testing, and LoQus expects to file a clinical trial application soon. Why it matters: Lilly has now completed more than a dozen acquisitions this year, including Calytrix Bio last week, and it keeps placing early bets in neuroscience alongside its larger deals for Centessa and AtaiBeckley.

Next, a major readout in cardiology. Novartis has reported positive results from ORION-4, its Phase three cardiovascular outcomes trial of Leqvio, inclisiran, according to a GuruFocus report on the company's announcement. The headline claim is a reduction in cardiovascular risk. The detailed figures were not available in the material we reviewed this morning, so the size of the benefit is the number to watch when the full data are presented. Why it matters: for a cholesterol lowering medicine, an outcomes trial is the evidence that shows fewer heart attacks and strokes rather than better lab values alone. A clear win gives Novartis a stronger case with doctors and payers, and it sets a reference point that competing cholesterol drugs will be measured against.

Turning to infectious disease. Invivyd says its antibody VYD2311, generic name ibramvibart, met all interim primary and secondary endpoints in DECLARATION, a Phase three trial for preventing symptomatic COVID nineteen, according to the company's press release. The interim analysis covered about two thousand three hundred fifty people given one injection, monthly injections, or placebo. One caution: COVID cases in the trial are still blinded, so the claim rests on antibody levels that the company says predict seventy to ninety percent protection over ninety days. Safety in both dosing arms tracked with placebo. Why it matters: Invivyd plans to seek accelerated approval in the first quarter of twenty twenty seven, a test of whether regulators will accept antibody levels as the basis for approving a preventive antibody.

Finally, a late stage start in pain. Latigo Biotherapeutics has begun a Phase three trial of onzotrigine, an oral NaV1.8 inhibitor for acute pain, according to its press release. About four hundred eighty adults undergoing bunion surgery will get onzotrigine, placebo, or oxycodone with acetaminophen. The main goal is pain relief over forty eight hours versus placebo, as in its earlier abdominoplasty study, with opioid sparing as a secondary goal. Topline results are expected in the second half of twenty twenty seven. Behind it sits a second NaV1.8 candidate, LTG-321, aimed at chronic musculoskeletal pain. Why it matters: NaV1.8 blockers aim to relieve pain without the addiction risk of opioids, and the opioid comparator lets Latigo show how its pill stacks up against what surgeons prescribe today.

The thread today is evidence: Lilly is buying early science, while Novartis, Invivyd and Latigo are each working to prove what their medicines can deliver in large trials and before regulators.
