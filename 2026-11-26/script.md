# Pharma and Biotech Daily, 2026-11-26

Good morning from Pharma and Biotech Daily, the briefing on what matters in pharma and biotech. It's Thursday, November 26.

Today, Washington proposes a new round of tariffs that singles out pharmaceutical exporters, and Bayer goes to China for a next generation lung cancer drug.

We start with trade. The Office of the US Trade Representative announced on Wednesday proposed tariffs on goods from fourteen economies under Section 301 of the Trade Act, as reported by Becker's Hospital Review. The agency argues these economies run structural excess capacity in manufacturing, and it named pharmaceuticals and medical devices among the sectors of concern. The proposed ceilings are fifteen percent for the European Union, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland, twenty percent for China, twenty five percent for India and thirty five percent for Vietnam. The trade office called European practices especially problematic in pharmaceuticals and pointed to Swiss public investment and fiscal incentives for the sector. Nothing takes effect yet. Comments are due January fifth and hearings follow on January twenty fifth. Pharmaceuticals were exempted from an earlier Section 301 action in July, so the annexes listing exclusions will be the document to read for anyone with supply in Europe, India or Switzerland.

Next, dealmaking. Bayer has taken an exclusive global license, outside Greater China, to HS twenty one twenty two from Hansoh Pharma, according to Bayer's own announcement. The drug is a bispecific antibody drug conjugate aimed at both EGFR and c MET, currently in Phase one in advanced solid tumors, with non small cell lung cancer as the lead opportunity. Bayer pays one hundred fifty million dollars upfront and up to three point one billion dollars in development, regulatory and sales milestones, plus tiered royalties. The agreement still needs antitrust clearance. Hansoh keeps adding big Western partners for its conjugates, and Bayer says the deal supports its precision oncology strategy of drugs that hit tumor cells more selectively.

Turning to the clinic. Bristol Myers Squibb reported mixed Phase three results for Sotyktu, deucravacitinib, in systemic lupus, as covered by the American Journal of Managed Care. In POETYK SLE one, with five hundred ten patients, the drug beat placebo on the SRI four response at week fifty two, and it also won on several secondary measures, including steroid tapering and fatigue. The sister trial, POETYK SLE two, missed its primary endpoint. The company said the drug's response looked consistent across both studies, but placebo performed better in the second. Safety matched what is known from psoriasis. Bristol has not released response rates, and it says it will review all its lupus data and talk to regulators, with no filing timeline given.

In patents, Amgen now has a route to launch its biosimilar of Opdivo in the United States, BioPharma Dive reports. A settlement with Bristol Myers Squibb and Ono Pharmaceutical lets Amgen enter before the relevant patents expire, with a launch date of October first, twenty twenty eight. That still depends on FDA approval, and Amgen expects the agency to respond to its application by the end of this year. The settlement terms are confidential. For Bristol, the deal puts a firm date on biosimilar competition for Opdivo, at a time when investors are already pressing the company on patent expirations. The same report notes Bristol's finance chief David Elkins will leave in March, with Kevin Dillon named to succeed him.

Finally, a supply warning. Pharming has paused production of Ruconest, its recombinant C one esterase inhibitor for acute attacks of hereditary angioedema, according to the company's release. A routine media fill, the simulation used to validate aseptic filling, failed at its third party fill and finish contractor. Pharming has notified the FDA and expects supply constraints in the United States, with current supply available through mid December. Production will restart only after the root cause is fixed and the process is requalified, and the company has not given a timeline. Product already with pharmacies and patients can still be used, and the company is working with the Hereditary Angioedema Association on access.

The thread today is exposure: to trade policy, to partners abroad, to a placebo arm or a single filling line, and how much each one can move a plan.
